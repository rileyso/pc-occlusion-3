# DCD baseline — text for the report

## Methodology (suggest as §5.x "Density-Aware Chamfer Distance Baseline")

To establish a geometric-similarity baseline against which the proposed visibility metric is compared, the Density-Aware Chamfer Distance (DCD) of Wu et al. [Wu et al., NeurIPS 2021] was adapted to object-level LiDAR observations. For each labelled pedestrian, the ideal point set was formed by uniformly area-weighted sampling (12 × 12 samples per face axis) of the LiDAR-facing faces of the ground-truth bounding box, following the box-surface approximation adopted in Section 5.8; back-facing surfaces were excluded so that self-occlusion is never penalised. The observed point set comprised the LiDAR returns inside the box. DCD was computed between the two sets using the official formulation (n_λ = 1, non-regularised mode disabled) at α = 1.0 on raw metre coordinates, an operating point chosen so that α·d² is of order one at the relevant nearest-surface distances; this mirrors the regime of the official implementation, which applies α = 1000 to unit-normalised shapes, and was selected through an α-sensitivity sweep. Plain Chamfer Distance was recorded alongside as a reference. Per-object DCD was computed for all 2,280 KITTI validation pedestrians (2,279 usable; one object returned no points) using a pure-PyTorch reimplementation of the official CUDA kernels, verified against a hand-computed reference case and structural property tests. Detection status was assigned by greedy confidence-ranked matching of each detector's validation predictions to ground-truth boxes at 3D IoU ≥ 0.50 with a confidence threshold of 0.1, and the relationship between DCD and detection probability was modelled with logistic regressions cubic in DCD, reported with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (200 resamples). Covariate-adjusted fits additionally included ground range and log point count, with both covariates held at their medians to expose the partial effect of DCD.

## Analysis (suggest following the results figure)

Figure X contrasts detection probability across the DCD scale for the four OpenPCDet detectors. The direction of the relationship is the opposite of what an occlusion metric requires: detection probability rises monotonically with DCD for every detector, with the detection rate increasing by 46.9 percentage points (PointPillars), 41.8 (SECOND), 67.6 (PointRCNN) and 42.3 (PV-RCNN) between the 10th and 95th DCD percentiles. The reason is that, in this application, DCD does not measure occlusion: across the 2,279 objects it correlates positively with the number of returns in the box (r = +0.63) and negatively with ground range (r = −0.71), because the size-ratio corrections and multiplicity weights of the official formulation assume point sets of comparable size, and are dominated by set-size asymmetry when a fixed ideal surface is compared with returns whose count varies from tens to more than a thousand. DCD therefore acts as a return-density proxy — rewarding precisely the close, densely scanned objects that are easiest to detect — rather than penalising coverage loss. After adjusting for range and point count, a reduced but non-zero association remains (+29.0, +9.1, +7.5 and +18.0 percentage points respectively), indicating some detection-relevant geometric information beyond density, although for PointRCNN the adjusted curve is nearly flat. The detector comparison is consistent with architecture expectations: PV-RCNN sustains the highest detection probability across the DCD scale, while PointRCNN is both weakest overall and the most dependent on dense returns. These results do not contradict DCD's value in its original completion and simulation-fidelity settings, where compared sets are of similar size; they show that the published formulation, applied per object with a variable observed set, fails the minimum requirement for an occlusion baseline — pointing in the wrong direction — and thereby substantiate the need for the dedicated object-level visibility metric developed in this thesis.

**Figure X.** Pedestrian detection probability versus per-object Density-Aware Chamfer Distance (DCD) on the KITTI validation split (n = 2,279) for four OpenPCDet detectors. Solid curves hold ground range and LiDAR point count at their medians (partial effect of DCD); dashed curves are unadjusted fits; shaded bands are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over 200 resamples. Detection is defined as a confidence-ranked prediction matching the ground-truth box at 3D IoU ≥ 0.50 with score ≥ 0.1. Detection probability increases with DCD for all detectors — the opposite of the direction an occlusion metric must exhibit — because DCD, under the official formulation with a variable observed point set, correlates with return density (r = +0.63 with point count, r = −0.71 with range) rather than with occlusion.
